Membership entitlement & access redesign

The CityFitness app showed whether a class could be booked, but not whether your membership included it. Testing with five gym members surfaced two mental-model problems. I changed the eligibility experience and the class information architecture in response.

The original interface exposed booking availability but not membership eligibility.

The original experience

Observed · usability testing

Booking didn't show eligibility

Observed · app walkthrough

Classes split across two places

Public information · checked 3 Oct 2026

Membership rules live elsewhere

CityFitness's public pages list Yoga and Pilates as Premium. Black Card is $14.99/week and Premium $18.99/week.

02

Usability testing

5 gym members with experience booking fitness classes took part. Testing focused on class discovery, membership eligibility, booking and navigation. It surfaced two separate mental-model problems.

Observed · usability testing
01

Bookable ≠ eligible

EligibilityMore than one Black Card participant
Mental model (paraphrased)If I can book it, my membership includes it.
BeforeObserved · testing

The original app let Black Card participants proceed with booking Yoga and Pilates. Nothing showed these needed Premium.

FindingObserved · testing

Black Card participants didn't infer that Yoga and Pilates required Premium. They took the working booking path as proof the class was included.

ChangeProposed

Show the membership requirement before the booking action. Black Card members see Premium membership required and can't book, but can still open the class and read its details.

AfterProposed

The upgrade path sits in context: Upgrade to Premium +NZ$4/week. The price is part of my proposed interface, based on the public weekly price difference. This creates a clearer upgrade path; its commercial effect is unvalidated.

02

Group Class ≠ all group classes

Information architectureOne Premium participant · a findability problem, separate from Finding 01
Mental model (paraphrased)Group Class = all group fitness classes.
BeforeObserved · app walkthrough

Yoga and Pilates were missing from the Group Class module and appeared under Explore.

FindingObserved · testing

A Premium participant expected Yoga and Pilates under Group Class. Finding them under Explore conflicted with their understanding of the category.

ChangeProposed

Bring all group classes into one discovery location, with membership eligibility shown next to each class.

AfterProposed

One schedule answers two questions together: what is the class, and can my membership access it. The member doesn't need to know the app's internal categories.

No screenshots of the original app are shown. “Before” describes what participants encountered. Findings are paraphrased from my session notes; there are no direct quotes. The screens are still being updated to the Premium naming. Membership pricing shown in the redesign reflects CityFitness's publicly listed pricing as of October 2026; the membership-compare screen export still shows earlier placeholder prices and is being updated.

Observed · my own audit

Blocked state isn't colour alone

Lock icon, dashed border and the words “Premium membership required”. The greyscale check is with the nine states in Detail D.

Touch targets

The original in-row Book button is about 53 × 33 pt. Prototype buttons are at least 44 pt tall.

Disabled vs blocked

No greyed-out Book button. The action is replaced by the reason and a next step.

Focus and semantics

2 px focus ring on every action. Each row reads as one sentence ending in its state and action.

06

Implementation considerations

Questions an engineer would need to answer. Not reviewed by engineering; CityFitness's actual architecture is unknown.

Not reviewed by engineering

One rule for eligibility

Proposed rule: schedule, class detail and the door read the same required-tier value. class.requiredTier is a conceptual model, not CityFitness's schema.

When eligibility can't load

Proposed: don't offer Book until eligibility is known, and show an error state instead. Would require engineering review.

After an upgrade

The product would need a reliable way to refresh eligibility after a membership upgrade; caching behaviour would need engineering review.

Evidence, assumptions & constraints

+

What is observed, assumed, proposed and still unvalidated.
What I observed

Public information, my walkthrough, testing

  • Public membership pages. CityFitness lists Basic, Black Card and Premium. Yoga and Pilates are part of Premium, not Black Card. Black Card is $14.99/week and Premium $18.99/week.
  • Current app. My walkthrough of booking and class detail.
  • Testing, finding 01. More than one Black Card participant read the booking path as proof that a class was included.
  • Testing, finding 02. One Premium participant expected Yoga and Pilates under Group Class.

Checked 3 Oct 2026: cityfitness.co.nz/memberships · help centre

Assumptions

Reasonable, not verified

  • Architecture. How booking and studio-door access are connected internally hasn't been verified. I modelled them as separate checks.
  • Door hardware. Doors already read a membership tier and would stay as they are.
  • Capacity. Premium classes have limited spots, so an ineligible booking costs a real place.
  • Roles. The six staff and member roles below.
Proposed

My design decisions

  • One entitlement model read by booking and door access.
  • Separate View, Book and Attend states.
  • A nine-state access component on every class row and class detail.
  • Fail closed: if access can't be confirmed, Book isn't offered.
Not yet validated

Hypotheses

  • how often booking and the studio door actually disagree;
  • whether tier rules are held separately by booking and door systems;
  • how much support or front-desk work refusals create;
  • whether the redesign fixes either finding (the redesigned experience has not yet been retested with users);
  • whether the upgrade path changes upgrade decisions;
  • whether the door can read a class-level tier.

—

Status never relies on colour

Each of the nine states has its own icon and words. The greyscale check in Detail D shows they still read.

Touch targets

The in-row “Book” button measures about 53 × 33 pt. That passes WCAG 2.2 AA (24 × 24) but is below Apple's 44 × 44 pt guideline. Specimens use a 44 pt minimum.

Plain-language messages

“Not available” becomes “Your Black Card won't open the Hot studio.” Errors say what happened and what still works: “Booking is paused so you don't book a class your card can't open.”

Screen-reader names

Each row reads as one sentence: class, time, state, then the action, e.g. “Hot Power Yoga, 7 AM, 60 minutes. Needs Premium. Compare memberships.” Booking results are announced politely.

Focus and large text

A 2 px focus ring with an offset on every action. At about 200% text the row stacks and the action goes full width instead of truncating.

Predictable, lower load

One action per row, always in the same place. A disabled Book button is replaced by the reason, so nobody taps a dead control.

Next round of testing

+

Plan for testing the redesign itself.
Who to recruit
  • 5 members again, mixing Black Card and Premium
  • 1–2 front-desk staff, if a club agrees
Tasks on the redesign
  • Find a class you can book tonight
  • You want a Yoga class. What does the app tell you, and what would you do?
  • Leave the upgrade screen without upgrading

Questions for staff
  • How often do you open a studio door manually, and why?
  • How do you explain a refused card today?
Assumptions to check
  • Booking/door mismatches happen at all
  • Restricted rows are noticed before tapping
  • Members find Back to leave the upgrade sheet
  • The door can read a class-level tier
What would change the design
  • Restrictions still missed → stronger grouping, or “I can book” as the default filter

  • Door can't read class tier → model access per studio

  • Five sessions show what confuses people, not how often
  • Front-desk input is one club's view
